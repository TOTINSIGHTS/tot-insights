Projects

TOT Insights hosts dedicated project pages for multi-output research strands, funded investigations, and advisory engagements. Each project brings together analytical outputs, datasets, and team information for a specific research line.

Active projects and engagements
Active 2024 –
Youth Militarisation in Russian-Occupied Ukraine

Comprehensive research programme mapping Russia's systematic militarisation of children and young people in Russian-occupied Ukrainian territories. Maps a five-tier architecture from preschool indoctrination through federally funded Voin training centres, with an estimated 600,000–650,000 children in the pipeline. The cohort reaches conscription age between 2028 and 2034. Produces analytical briefings, interactive data tools, and primary source collections on the architecture of child militarisation and its long-term implications for occupied populations and regional security.

Partners
Leverhulme Centre for Research on Slavery in War, King's College London  ·  Centre for Statecraft and National Security, KCL
Active 2024 –
Forms of Militarisation in Russian-Occupied Ukraine

Research strand examining the broader architecture of militarisation in Russian-occupied Ukraine beyond youth programming. Covers conscription and passportisation as entrapment mechanisms, forced labour and mobilisation under occupation, the conversion of civilian and educational infrastructure to military purposes, and the administrative systems that sustain military mobilisation across the occupied oblasts. Produces primary source collections, monitoring data, and analytical outputs on the mechanisms of military integration under occupation.

Partners
Centre for Statecraft and National Security, King's College London  ·  Leverhulme Centre for Research on Slavery in War, KCL
Active 2024 –
Settler Colonialism in Russian-Occupied Ukraine

Research strand applying settler colonial theory to Russia's civilian relocation programme across the four occupied oblasts and Crimea. Documents interlocking mechanisms: Zemsky relocation schemes (budget doubled year-on-year through at least 2027), civil administrator co-option, Rosatom and federal ministry placements, and organic migration of construction workers and ideologically motivated individuals. Over 80 named Russian civilian settlers identified through open-source data. Argues the programme constitutes a structural elimination of Ukrainian society through transfer, assimilation, and demographic displacement.

Partners
Centre for Statecraft and National Security, King's College London  ·  Centre for Occupation Studies
Advisory 2024 –
Lessons from Ukrainian Resistance — SONAC Advisory

Advisory contribution to research on the effectiveness of civilian and non-violent resistance in Russian-occupied Ukrainian territories. Draws on field research, interviews across free Ukraine, and primary source collections to assess what forms of resistance have survived intensified Russian surveillance and control, and what the structural shift toward compartmentation and trust-based networks means for occupied populations, their supporters, and for international thinking on civilian agency under occupation. Feeds into policy recommendations for Ukraine and Western partner governments.
