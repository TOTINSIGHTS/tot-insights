About TOT Insights

TOT Insights is a research platform of the Ukraine and Russia Programme at the Centre for Statecraft and National Security, in cooperation with the Leverhulme Centre for Research on Slavery in War, King's College London. It publishes analytical briefings, interactive data tools, datasets, and primary source collections on Russian occupation mechanisms, civilian entrapment, and accountability in Russian-occupied Ukrainian territories.

The platform's purpose is to define and develop occupation studies as a field of scholarly inquiry, using the Russian occupation of Ukrainian territories as the primary empirical case. Its work is organised around three core research questions: what is the structure and logic of Russian occupation governance, and how does Russia control civilian populations through legal, economic, identity, and digital mechanisms? How do civilian populations navigate and resist coercive rule under conditions of entrapment, and what forms of agency and circumvention exist? And what are the long-term consequences of this model of occupation — for the territories and their populations, for Ukrainian sovereignty, and for international norms around occupation governance?

TOT Insights is a research platform, not an advocacy tool or operational programme. Its outputs are analytical: briefings, dashboards, datasets, and peer-reviewed scholarship. It draws on original fieldwork in Ukraine, occupation administrative records, and open-source monitoring to support researchers, policymakers, and accountability practitioners. The platform is updated on a rolling basis as new research, data, and primary sources become available.

We are a collaborative platform and are always open to discussing ways of working together, whether through joint research, data sharing, or other forms of partnership. If you would like to get in touch, please visit our Contact page.

Kimberly Metcalf
Research Assistant — TOT Insights Hub

Kimberly Metcalf is a Ph.D. candidate in War Studies at King's College London and Research Assistant at the Centre for Statecraft and National Security's TOT Insights Hub, where her research examines Russian identity production, securitization, and Russia's systematic economic predation in Ukraine's Temporarily Occupied Territories. Her doctoral work is political anthropological in approach, centred on Russian identity in the context of securitization with an emphasis on the 'Hidden Transcript' and the 'Microphysics of Power,' grounded in qualitative fieldwork across Georgia, Kazakhstan, the Kyrgyz Republic, Montenegro, Israel, and other locations.

Her academic research is inseparable from two decades of operational experience. She worked the counter-ISIS problem set from 2014 to 2020, first as a U.S. Army Special Operations Civil Affairs Officer serving on the CJIATF-Syria, at the U.S. Embassy in Amman, and as Effects Director for the CJSOTF-Syria, before deploying to Kobanî as Civil Affairs Planner following the liberation of Manbij — where she coordinated with NGOs, facilitated demining efforts, conducted rapid infrastructure needs assessments of de-occupied spaces, advocated for displaced civilians to return home safely, assessed IDP camps, and supported the restoration of normal life in a de-occupied city. She subsequently led civilian analytical teams in Erbil (2019–2020) and Kyiv (2023–2025), managing analysts and research partners to support strategic communications planning for government stakeholders.

She has published in the Texas National Security Review, Small Wars Journal, War on the Rocks, and Inkstick Media, and holds an MA cum laude in European Union and Russian Studies from the University of Tartu (2020).
