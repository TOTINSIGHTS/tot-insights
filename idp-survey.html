Corrected October 2026. In the workbook first published here, the table for Q8 (“most of the population remaining in the occupied territories is satisfied with the Russian authorities”) repeated the figures for Q9 (hidden resistance) by mistake. The correct distribution is 26.2% definitely no, 32.4% rather no, 19.8% rather yes, 7.6% definitely yes, 13.8% hard to say and 0.2% prefer not to answer. The “hard to say” and “prefer not to answer” figures for Q7 were transposed (correct: 0.8% and 0.5%), and four multiple-choice figures were off by 0.1 points through rounding. The workbook above has been reissued with a corrections sheet; every value was rechecked against the respondent-level file. The dashboard's headline figures were not affected. The 2026 wave sets the corrected 2025 results alongside the new data.

About this dataset

A survey of 1,001 internally displaced persons (IDPs) from Russian-occupied Ukrainian territories, fielded in September 2025. This workbook publishes the aggregated results: one-way frequency distributions (with sample size and margin of error) and coded summaries of open-ended answers. Individual respondent-level records are not published. Responses and question wording are in Ukrainian; the 2026 wave page gives English translations of every 2025 question it repeats.

What the survey covers

  • Displacement status and IDP self-identification
  • Demographics, including age category and sex
  • Ratings of institutional effectiveness, including the Ukrainian government
  • Contact with people and conditions in Russian-occupied territories
  • Attitudes toward Russian rule in the occupied territories
  • Assessments of non-violent resistance actions, and what support is worth the risk

Explore the findings

For an interactive breakdown of the results, see the IDP Attitudes Survey dashboard. Among the headline figures: 76.4% rate the Ukrainian government ineffective, and 61.6% believe hidden resistance exists in the occupied territories.
