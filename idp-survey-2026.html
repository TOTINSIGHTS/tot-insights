Correction to the 2025 results. The 2025 table for the statement that most people remaining in the occupied territories are satisfied with the Russian authorities repeated, in error, the figures for the hidden-resistance question. The correct 2025 distribution is 26.2% definitely no, 32.4% rather no, 19.8% rather yes and 7.6% definitely yes (58.5% disagree, 27.4% agree). Two smaller transpositions and rounding slips were also fixed. The 2025 workbook has been reissued, and the corrected figures are used throughout this page.

Key findings

  • Fewer think the remaining population is content with Russian rule. 15.6% agree that most people who stayed are satisfied with the Russian authorities, down from 27.4% in 2025 (19.0% among respondents from occupied settlements). 63.5% disagree.
  • Belief in hidden resistance has softened, and uncertainty has grown. 51.8% say there is hidden resistance to the occupiers (55.2% in the comparable subsample), against 61.6% in 2025. "Hard to say" rose from 20.4% to 28.2%.
  • Displaying Ukrainian identity is seen as the greatest risk. Asked for the first time, 54.9% name using Ukrainian symbols and 50.3% name refusing a Russian passport as behaviour that can provoke violence from the Russian authorities.
  • Property loss is widespread. Among respondents in contact with the occupied territories whose family had property there, 45.0% report it destroyed or damaged and 10.3% report it declared "ownerless" and transferred to the occupation authorities; 28.3% know of "ownerless" seizures among relatives or acquaintances.
  • Strong support for strikes on military targets. 75.0% consider Ukrainian strikes on targets in the occupied territories justified, with military bases and ammunition depots the top priorities and facilities in residential areas chosen by 1.5%.
  • Return and prejudice. 55.7% of those from still-occupied settlements plan to return after de-occupation. 45.0% have faced prejudice as displaced people, most often in everyday communication and when renting housing.
  • Assessments of the Ukrainian government are little changed. 72.5% rate its support for IDPs ineffective (76.6% comparable), against 76.4% in 2025.

Reading the comparison with 2025

Both waves were fielded by telephone by the same company with around 1,000 respondents, but the samples differ. In 2025 every respondent came from a settlement that had been occupied. The 2026 sample is broader: 24.4% come from settlements that have never been occupied, and all respondents consider themselves displaced. Comparisons can therefore reflect who was asked as well as changes in opinion. Tables with a 2025 figure also show a 2026 comparable column restricted to the 725 respondents whose pre-invasion settlement was occupied at some point, and the change column compares that subsample with 2025. Contact with the occupied territories illustrates the difference: 37.6% of the full 2026 sample keep in touch, against 47.8% of the comparable subsample and 48.6% in 2025.

The comparable subsample is a TOT Insights calculation from the respondent-level file; all other figures are as supplied by the pollster and were checked against that file. Question numbers differ between waves; each table lists its 2025 equivalent.

Method

  • Computer-assisted telephone interviews (CATI) with 1,007 internally displaced persons, September 2026, by OPERSO - Operatyvna Sotsiolohiia.
  • Respondents originate mainly from Donetsk (56.7%), Kharkiv (14.6%), Kherson (11.0%), Zaporizhzhia and Luhansk oblasts (6.9% each); 2.6% now live abroad.
  • Margin of error at 95% confidence: 3.1% for values near 50%, 2.7% near 25% or 75%, 1.4% near 5% or 95%. Margins are wider for questions asked of a subset; each table shows its base.
  • As fielded, questions 19 to 25 (media trust, living standards, property, shortages, information sources) were asked only of the 371 respondents in regular contact with acquaintances in the occupied territories.
  • Open-ended "other" answers were translated into English by the pollster and are listed under the relevant tables. Individual respondent records are not published.
