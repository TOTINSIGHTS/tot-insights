Russian Security Law in the Occupied Territories: Seven Articles of the Criminal and Administrative Codes, the Cases Brought under Them, and 22 Martial-Law and Security Acts

Territory
Russian Federation; applied in occupied Crimea and Sevastopol since 2014 and in the four regions annexed in 2022
Issuers
Federal Assembly and President of the Russian Federation; occupation authorities of Zaporizhzhia and Kherson oblasts, the "LPR", Crimea and Sevastopol
Theme
Accountability and Legal · Coercion, Entrapment and Slavery · Civilian Life and Resistance
Format
Primary Source collection · 7 articles with English translations, linked to the Civilian Detainees Database (DOC-182) · 22 federal and occupation-authority acts in Russian with English summaries

Russian courts apply the federal Criminal Code and Code of Administrative Offences in every territory Russia claims, including occupied Crimea since 2014 and the Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson regions since 2022. Each page in this collection carries the Russian text of one article with a working English translation. Each row below also links to the people in the Civilian Detainees Database charged under that article.

Treason (Art. 275) and espionage (Art. 276) dominate the case record: the database lists 527 people charged with treason and 327 with espionage, against 16 under the confidential-cooperation article added in July 2022 and 5 under assisting the enemy. The median sentence in treason cases is 14 years. The administrative articles on "discrediting" the army and banned symbols appear mostly in occupied Crimea. The counts are a floor: only 1,832 of the database's 4,384 records name the articles charged, and a case can cite several articles, so a sentence counted under one article may cover others too.

The seven articles

Criminal CodeArt. 275 High treasonCriminalises high treason by a citizen of the Russian Federation, espionage, handing over state secrets, "going over to the enemy's side," or providing assistance to a foreign state against Russian security. 12–20 years or life. The pivotal charge once passportisation is factored in: taking a Russian passport is what makes contact with Ukraine chargeable as treason. In the detainee database52714 years (median of 358 sentences) Criminal CodeArt. 275.1 Confidential cooperation with a foreign state or organisationThe catch-all (added July 2022). Criminalises merely "establishing and maintaining" a confidential relationship with a foreign state or organisation to assist activity against Russian security, no transfer of secrets required. Reaches both Russian citizens and non-citizens, closing the gaps left by Articles 275, 276 and 276.1. 3–8 years. In the detainee database165.5 years (median of 12 sentences) Criminal CodeArt. 276 EspionageEspionage by a foreign citizen or stateless person. A 2022 amendment added the transfer or gathering, in armed conflict, of information usable against the Russian Armed Forces, covering the passing of coordinates or troop positions to Ukraine. The non-citizen counterpart to treason: passport status changes the article, not the exposure. 10–20 years. In the detainee database32712 years (median of 258 sentences) Criminal CodeArt. 276.1 Assisting the enemyThe newest addition (December 2024) and cleanest "aiding the enemy" offence: financial, material, consultative or "other" assistance to the enemy by a non-citizen, with no transfer of information required. Completes the non-citizen half of the scheme and marks how the collaboration net tightened in late 2024. 10–15 years. In the detainee database511 years (median of 5 sentences) Criminal CodeArt. 280.4 Public calls to activity against state securityA speech offence (introduced 2023). Criminalises public calls against Russian security or to obstruct the authorities, reaching pro-Ukrainian statements, calls for liberation and encouragement of resistance, especially online. Sits above the administrative discrediting charge as the criminal escalation. Fine or 2–4 years, up to 6–7 for organised forms. In the detainee database123.5 years (median of 10 sentences) Administrative CodeArt. 20.3 Display of banned symbolsThe everyday tool for policing Ukrainian identity. Penalises display of "banned" symbols; the phrase "or other symbols whose display is banned by federal law" sweeps in the Azov, UPA and Right Sector emblems, and in practice the Ukrainian trident and flag. Fine with confiscation or up to 15 days' arrest; routinely stacked with CoAP 20.3.3. In the detainee database10Administrative offence: fines or short arrest Administrative CodeArt. 20.3.3 "Discrediting" the armed forcesThe workhorse administrative charge for dissent (introduced March 2022). Captures any public criticism of the war, support for Ukraine or objection to mobilisation. A first offence is administrative; a repeat within a year escalates to Criminal Code Art. 280.3. Court practice already runs in occupied Crimea. In the detainee database23Administrative offence: fines or short arrest

Martial-law and security acts for the occupied territories

Federal acts written for the occupied territories, and acts of the occupation authorities that set curfews, entry and exit regimes, expulsion and resettlement, seizures, bans on churches, assemblies and photography. Each links to the official Russian text; the English titles and summaries are TOT Insights' own, checked against the text. Acts are filed under the names their issuers use, which describes the source and does not recognise it.

19 October 2022Presidential Decree No. 756On introducing martial law in the territories of the DPR, LPR, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson regionsIntroduces martial law in the four regions and orders the Government to propose implementing measures on the advice of the defence and interior ministries, the FSB and other security bodies.Russian text (PDF) · official source
19 October 2022Presidential Decree No. 757On measures taken in federal subjects in connection with Decree No. 756Sets the "maximum response level" in the four regions, with territorial defence and territorial-defence headquarters, and the "medium response level" in Crimea, Sevastopol and six Russian border regions.Russian text (PDF) · official source
27 April 2023Presidential Decree No. 307On the specifics of the legal status of certain categories of foreign citizens and stateless persons in the Russian FederationTreats residents of the four regions who kept Ukrainian citizenship or did not take the Russian citizenship oath as foreigners, with Ukrainian residence documents valid only until 1 July 2024. Point 13 provides for deportation, readmission, administrative expulsion or entry bans for those deemed a threat to public order or security, including people taking part in unauthorised rallies.Russian text (PDF) · official source
4 March 2026Presidential Decree No. 140Amending the procedures approved by Decree No. 951 of 26 December 2022 and Decree No. 307 of 27 April 2023Deletes the words "before the end of the transition period" from the citizenship procedures set by Decree No. 951 for the four regions, and amends Decree No. 307.Russian text (PDF)
29 May 2023Federal Constitutional Law No. 2-FKZAmending certain federal constitutional laws (martial-law provisions)Adds to the martial-law law administrative detention of up to 30 days for breaching presidential martial-law restrictions, and the "forced and controlled relocation" of citizens from territory under martial law to territory without it.Russian text (PDF) · official source
31 July 2023Federal Law No. 395-FZOn applying the Criminal Code and Criminal Procedure Code in the DPR, LPR, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson regionsApplies Russian criminal law to acts committed in the four regions before 30 September 2022. Article 2 provides that an act is not a crime if it "was aimed at protecting the interests" of the Russian Federation, the DPR or the LPR.Russian text (PDF) · official source
