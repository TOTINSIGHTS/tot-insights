Russia made "History of Our Region" a compulsory part of school history in Years 5 to 7 from 1 September 2025 (Ministry of Education Order No. 704 of 9 October 2024). For the occupied Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson regions the Ministry has admitted a federal textbook for the course (DOC-171); in Crimea it follows this regional programme, and the occupation ministry is preparing Crimean textbooks to match. The programme states its purpose as forming "all-Russian civic identity" and showing "the significance of Crimea in the history of Russia". Its 68 hours open with "the Russian World" and Russian as the only language named, frame the Crimean Khanate chiefly through the slave trade, and teach 1783 as Catherine II "accepting" the peninsula. In Year 7, seven of the seventeen lessons go to the Second World War, while the 1944 deportation appears as a single line with no date, no perpetrator and no mention of the Crimean Tatars. A single lesson covers 1945 to 1991 and declares the 1954 transfer to the Ukrainian SSR illegitimate. No lesson covers 1991 to 2014: the programme never says that Crimea was part of independent Ukraine, and moves straight to the 2014 "coup" in Kyiv, the "polite people" and the "reunification". The final project lesson teaches the official goals of the war, including the "defascisation" of Ukraine. It also has pupils aged 12 to 13 identify local participants and take part in volunteer groups that deliver "technical equipment" to soldiers.

Structure of the course

68 hours in total: 34 in Year 5 (prehistory to the end of the thirteenth century), 17 in Year 6 (fourteenth century to the early twentieth) and 17 in Year 7 (the twentieth century to the present). The Year 7 lesson plan allocates seven lessons to the Second World War, one to 1945–1991, none to 1991–2014, two to the "Crimean Spring" of 2014 and its aftermath, two to citizenship and the Crimean constitution, one project lesson to the war against Ukraine ("Special military operation. Crimeans who are heroes of the SVO") and one closing museum lesson. According to the occupation Ministry of Education, Crimean schools teach the course in the fourth term of the school year.

Note

The programme was written by staff of KRIPPO, the institute that retrains Crimea's teachers. Its senior author, Yu. M. Mogarichev, heads the institute's department of social and humanities education. One of its reviewers, V. V. Maiko, directs the Institute of Archaeology of Crimea of the Russian Academy of Sciences. The collegium decision on the first page recommends the programme to every general-education school in Crimea "with the aim of implementing unified approaches", so it sets out the standard version of the course that Crimean teachers are expected to follow.
