Original Russian-language document. Legislative amendment to Article 53 of the Luhansk People's Republic Law on Military Duty and Military Service. The LPR maintained a parallel body of military duty legislation to the DPR (see DOC-105, DOC-107); this document is the LPR counterpart to the DPR amendment (DOC-107), targeting the provisions governing grounds for non-conscription in Article 53 of the LPR military duty law. Sourced from the LPR legislative portal.

This law amends Article 53 of the Luhansk People's Republic Law on Military Duty and Military Service, which sets out the grounds on which LPR citizens are not subject to conscription call-up. Article 53 is the LPR equivalent of Article 23 of the DPR Law on Military Duty (DPR Law No. 08-INs; see DOC-105), and covers exemption grounds including health incapacity, custodial sentence, criminal investigation status, and other legislative provisions. The amendment modifies the scope of these grounds, tightening the criteria for exemption in a manner consistent with preparation for intensified military operations. Read alongside the DPR equivalent (DOC-107) and the LPR travel and conscription decrees (DOC-118, DOC-120), this amendment is part of a systematic sequence of legal adjustments that both the DPR and LPR undertook in the period before the general mobilisation of February 2022 and that continued after absorption into the Russian military system.

Analytical note — LPR military duty legislative sequence

The LPR's legislative architecture for military mobilisation mirrors the DPR's, with the same structural components: a base Law on Military Duty and Military Service; periodic amendments tightening exemption grounds; a separate Law on Mobilisation Preparation; and executive decrees (such as DOC-118) imposing travel restrictions and conscription-enforcement measures. The LPR and DPR legislative sequences were synchronised rather than developed independently, reflecting Russian oversight of the legislative process in both entities. The amendment to Article 53 is analytically significant as evidence of this synchronisation: similar amendments were introduced to the equivalent article (Article 23) of the DPR law in the same period (see DOC-107).
