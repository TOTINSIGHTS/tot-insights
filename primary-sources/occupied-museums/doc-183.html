How Russia absorbed the museums and heritage of occupied Ukraine, in its own documents. The collection gathers the laws, treaties, decrees, resolutions, ministry orders and letters that admitted the occupied territories to the Russian Federation, set up culture ministries and heritage bodies there, took Ukrainian state property, and brought local museums into the Museum Fund and the State Catalogue of the Russian Federation. Acts of the occupation authorities are filed under the names those authorities use for themselves. That describes the source; it does not recognise it.

Each file is the act's own text, taken from the official publication where it could be reached and otherwise from a legal database or the copy posted on the State Catalogue's site (goskatalog.ru). 23 files are renderings of a saved web page, Word file or text and are marked as such; many others are scans without a text layer, so the PDF itself cannot be searched. CURIA Lab gathered the acts from five sources: the acts cited in two of its studies on Crimea and Donetsk region; the federal acts that govern the Museum Fund and the State Catalogue and the accession acts; searches of official sites in Sevastopol and the Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson regions; the State Catalogue's documents page; and the Donetsk acts portal. The list is as full as CURIA Lab could make it but has not been confirmed to be complete, and coverage is thinner for Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson regions than for federal acts, Crimea and Donetsk region.

    Notes on the files

    Files are named by date of signing, issuing authority and number. A Rendering flag means the file is a PDF made from a saved web page, Word file or text, with a box on its first page naming the act and the source. A Consolidated flag means the text is a later edition, not the act as signed. 19 files have no web address: they are copies posted on the State Catalogue's site, downloaded on 18 June 2025. Four acts in the set (Federal Constitutional Laws 5-FKZ and 8-FKZ, the Kherson accession treaty and Federal Law 375-FZ) were already on TOT Insights and open on their existing pages, with English key provisions. The other 176 files are served from the TOT Insights document archive.
