The System of Military-Ideological Training of Children in the Temporarily Occupied Territories of Ukraine

Centre for the Study of Occupation for TOT Insights. Analytical report, as of 17 September 2026. Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson regions; Crimea excluded.

Key conclusion

As of September 2026, Russia has built a multi-layered system of military-ideological work with children in the occupied parts of Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson regions. Ordinary schools and mass youth movements form the base; Yunarmiya, Zarnitsa 2.0, cadet classes and the VOIN Centre provide progressively more specialised layers.

The main shift in 2024-2026 is the merger of programmes that initially operated in parallel. Movement of the First, Yunarmiya and VOIN now jointly run Zarnitsa 2.0; OBZR moves elements of initial military training into mandatory schooling; and the ideological component reaches primary school and, in pilot form, preschool age. source

The appropriate unit of analysis is not membership in a single organisation but the entire pathway of a child: preschool ideological socialisation → Eaglets of Russia → Movement of the First → OBZR and extracurricular work → Zarnitsa 2.0 → Yunarmiya/cadet classes → VOIN. These audiences partly overlap and must not be added into a single total.

Methodology and data limitations

This report relies on Russian federal, regional and occupation-controlled sources, official education documents, materials published by the organisations themselves, and prior CSO analytical work. Ukrainian sources were not used for the quantitative section.

Russian statistics are methodologically inconsistent. Different releases count registered members, activists, event participants, trainees, or cumulative numbers of people who have completed a course. VOIN statistics cover ages 14-35; the Yunarmiya charter used in occupied Donetsk also permits participation up to age 35. These figures therefore cannot be read as direct counts of children. VOIN Yunarmiya

Yunarmiya in occupied Donetsk illustrates the problem. Local sources reported about 5,500 participants in May-June 2023. After the local movement joined the all-Russia structure and underwent re-registration, the official September 2024 figure was 4,100. This break in the series is more consistent with a change in the registration base than with a real contraction. 2023 2024 integration

Evolution of the system in 2022-2026

In 2022-2023 the emphasis was on visible nodes: Yunarmiya detachments, military-patriotic clubs, oath ceremonies, trips and camps. In Mariupol, the first Yunarmiya detachment was created on 9 May 2022 while fighting for Azovstal was still underway. DAN

In 2023-2024 institutionalisation accelerated. Movement of the First expanded school branches, the local Young Guard - Yunarmiya structure was integrated into the all-Russia Yunarmiya, and VOIN branches opened in all four occupied regions in spring 2024. Yunarmiya integration VOIN

From the 2024/25 school year, the military component entered ordinary schooling through OBZR, training camps, specialised classrooms, cadet classes and agency-linked profile classes. Zarnitsa 2.0 simultaneously moved toward UAV, sapper, medic, communications and assault specialisations. OBZR Zarnitsa 2.0

By 2025-2026, the programmes function as an interconnected chain. Separate brands remain, but their operators, infrastructure and event calendars increasingly overlap.

Architecture of a child’s involvement

The lower layers provide mass coverage; the upper layers provide selection and specialisation. A child does not need to join Yunarmiya to encounter military-ideological content regularly: ordinary schooling already includes OBZR, Conversations about Important Things, Lessons of Courage, Movement of the First activities and school stages of Zarnitsa.

No separate systemic “Sokolyata” programme has been verified in the occupied territories excluding Crimea. The name appears as a local designation for individual groups but not as a network comparable to Eaglets of Russia, Movement of the First or Yunarmiya. It is therefore excluded from the quantitative model.

Eaglets of Russia: primary-school entry layer

Eaglets of Russia is a federal extracurricular programme for grades 1-4 and functions as the lowest mass school layer of the Russian youth system. official site

In the occupied part of Zaporizhzhia region, by the start of the 2025/26 school year the programme operated in 97 schools, with 626 class-teams and 10,154 pupils. regional programme

In May 2026, children from all four occupied regions took part in a federal Eaglets of Russia session that brought together 714 fourth-graders from 34 Russian regions and occupied territories. Eaglets of Russia

On 1 September 2026, more than 500 first-graders in occupied Donetsk were inducted into Eaglets of Russia in ceremonies across all municipalities. This is not a total membership figure but it shows annual replenishment at the youngest school-age level. DAN

Movement of the First: the mass layer

Movement of the First is not formally a military organisation. Its function is to provide a mass school network through which children are channelled into federal competitions, camps, Zarnitsa 2.0, meetings with military personnel and other “patriotic” programmes.

In occupied Zaporizhzhia, the organisation reported more than 14,000 participants by the end of 2024 and more than 23,000 activists by the end of 2025. Occupation sources themselves described annual growth of roughly 40%. There were 189 primary branches in educational institutions at the start of the 2025/26 school year. regional programme

In occupied Donetsk, by July 2026 Movement of the First reported 27,000 participants and publicly set the goal of involving every second schoolchild, with up to 1,000 new participants added each month. DAN, 9 July 2026

In occupied Kherson, the regional branch reported more than 1,500 events and 47 projects during 2025. Network growth increasingly connects general youth activity with projects jointly operated by military-patriotic structures. Kherson

Zarnitsa 2.0: transition to military specialisations

Zarnitsa 2.0 is the clearest point where previously separate structures merge. It is jointly organised by Movement of the First, Yunarmiya and the VOIN Centre, while occupation education authorities provide school and municipal stages. Zaporizhzhia Luhansk

In occupied Zaporizhzhia, participation rose from roughly 860 in 2024 to more than 2,000 in 2025. In occupied Kherson, the figure rose from more than 600 to more than 1,200 participants from 60 educational institutions. The 2026 target in Zaporizhzhia was again at least 2,000.

The federal format includes UAV operator, combat engineer/sapper, medic, communications specialist, assault role, commander and military correspondent tracks. The middle age category begins at 11; the senior category covers ages 14-17. federal final

In occupied Luhansk, an education ministry document explicitly defines the acquisition and consolidation of initial military-training skills as an objective of the game. Military specialisation is therefore embedded in the normative design of the programme. order

Yunarmiya: the permanent organisational core

Yunarmiya has lower mass coverage than the broad school movements but provides a permanent organisational environment: detachments, local staffs, oath ceremonies, uniforms, Yunarmiya Houses, field camps, initial military training and specialist tracks.

Occupied Donetsk: revised membership data

In May 2023, local sources reported 5,500 activists in Young Guard - Yunarmiya. In June the procedure for joining the all-Russia Yunarmiya began; from July 2023 the structure operated as a regional branch while retaining the local brand. 18 May 2023 5 June 2023

On 10 September 2024, regional chief Viktor Pudak reported 4,100 participants and more than 150 detachments, with an increase of 1,091 since the start of the year. By 26 November the total had exceeded 5,000; 630 people took the oath during 2024. 10 Sep 2024 26 Nov 2024

In May 2025 occupation authorities reported more than 5,500 activists in 17 municipalities. During 2025, 1,250 new participants joined; the exact stated total on 28 January 2026 was 6,347. 11 May 2025 28 Jan 2026

On 28 May 2026 Pudak said the regional branch had “approached 7,000”. An anniversary post by the movement dated 5 April simultaneously claimed more than 7,000 members, 190 detachments in 18 municipalities, seven Yunarmiya Houses and more than 800 people receiving initial military training there. Because these two official statements conflict, this report uses a conservative current estimate of about 7,000. 28 May 2026 5 Apr 2026

At the start of 2026 six Yunarmiya Houses were officially operating; the leadership announced a plan to open another 14 and raise the network to 20 permanent centres. Infrastructure expansion is as significant as membership growth because it converts the movement from a network of detachments into a network of permanent training sites. DAN

Mariupol

The first Yunarmiya detachment in Mariupol was created on 9 May 2022. By May 2025, Russian sources reported around 10 detachments and more than 200 active participants. A Yunarmiya House opened in 2024 and received a RUB 1.2 million equipment certificate in 2025. DAN

Mariupol’s trajectory from a single symbolic detachment to a city network within three years illustrates the expansion model in newly occupied areas: a visible initial node, followed by a local staff, multiple detachments, a permanent facility and integration into the regional structure.

Occupied Luhansk and Zaporizhzhia

In occupied Luhansk, Russian sources repeatedly cited more than 6,000 Yunarmiya members in late 2024 and 2025, but the figure includes graduates and older age groups and should not be read as a count of active schoolchildren. In occupied Zaporizhzhia, as of 1 September 2025 there were 28 detachments with 355 participants. Zaporizhzhia

VOIN Centre: advanced applied military training

VOIN branches in the four occupied regions opened in spring 2024. By 11 March 2026 they reported 10,381 trained participants: 3,708 in occupied Donetsk, 3,126 in occupied Luhansk, 1,935 in occupied Zaporizhzhia and 1,612 in occupied Kherson. VOIN Centre

Programmes include firearms and tactical training, engineering, UAVs, first aid, tactical medicine, survival, reconnaissance and sniper training. The age bracket of 14-35 means that the 10,381 total is not a child-only figure, although specific programmes and camps explicitly target ages 14-17. source

The child segment is visible in the Time of Young Heroes camp system. Across the entire VOIN network, participation of 14-17-year-olds rose from 9,158 in 2023 to 10,905 in 2024 and 12,935 in 2025. These all-Russia camp figures must not be added to the 10,381 regional total, but they show the growth rate of the youth component.

Cadet and security-agency profile classes

In occupied Zaporizhzhia, as of September 2025 there were 11 cadet classes with 279 pupils and four profile classes linked to the Investigative Committee, Emergencies Ministry and Interior Ministry with 108 pupils, for a total of 387 schoolchildren. regional programme

This layer differs from Yunarmiya because orientation toward Russian security institutions is embedded directly into the school structure. For some children, a military-security career track becomes part of formal education rather than an extracurricular activity.

Mandatory school layer: OBZR and training camps

From 1 September 2024, Russia replaced the former life-safety course with Fundamentals of Security and Defence of the Motherland (OBZR). In occupied Luhansk it is mandatory in grades 8-9 and includes training camps; it is also part of the senior-school curriculum. curriculum

In occupied Donetsk, Russian authorities reported equipping OBZR classrooms in 513 schools during 2025. In occupied Zaporizhzhia, the regional education plan includes an OBZR Week, training camps for senior pupils and students, Lessons of Courage and other military-patriotic activities. Donetsk Zaporizhzhia

Through OBZR, basic elements of military preparation no longer depend on voluntary membership in Yunarmiya or VOIN. They reach ordinary pupils through the mandatory educational process.

Extracurricular military-ideological work

Outside formal lessons, the system is reinforced through weekly Conversations about Important Things, Lessons of Courage, meetings with Russian servicemen and war participants, “Hero Desks”, school museums and “SVO” exhibits, drill competitions, field camps, first-aid and tactical-medicine activities, shooting-related training and aid campaigns for Russian forces.

In occupied Donetsk, Conversations about Important Things are embedded in the regional education programme. In the 2025/26 school year the region was also included in a pilot for children from age five; one of the pilot institutions was kindergarten No. 98 in Mariupol. schools preschool pilot

The preschool layer is not direct military training. Its function is early Russian identity and historical-political socialisation, after which the child moves into Eaglets of Russia and the school network of Movement of the First.

Regional patterns

Occupied Donetsk

Occupied Donetsk has the most developed permanent Yunarmiya core: about 7,000 participants by a conservative mid-2026 estimate, up to 190 detachments and a growing network of Yunarmiya Houses. In parallel, Movement of the First reports 27,000 participants and an explicit goal of enrolling every second schoolchild. The result is a two-layer structure: a mass civilian-youth network and a narrower military-patriotic core. Yunarmiya Movement of the First

Occupied Luhansk

Occupied Luhansk is characterised by particularly formalised school regulation: Zarnitsa 2.0 and OBZR are embedded in education documents, while Movement of the First is designed for coverage across the school network. The school channel is therefore central. Movement of the First Zarnitsa OBZR

Occupied Zaporizhzhia

Occupied Zaporizhzhia provides the clearest quantitative cross-section of the full vertical: 10,154 Eaglets of Russia; more than 23,000 Movement of the First activists by the end of 2025; more than 2,000 Zarnitsa 2.0 participants; 355 Yunarmiya members; 387 pupils in cadet and security-agency profile classes; and 1,935 VOIN trainees by March 2026. These indicators represent different categories and partly overlapping audiences. regional programme VOIN

Occupied Kherson

Occupied Kherson shows rapid expansion through mass-event formats: Zarnitsa 2.0 roughly doubled its participation between 2024 and 2025, while Movement of the First expanded its network of primary branches. The VOIN branch reported 1,612 trained participants by March 2026. Zarnitsa Movement of the First VOIN

Dynamics and scale comparison

The largest absolute numbers belong to the mass organisations rather than specialised military structures. In occupied Zaporizhzhia, Movement of the First has tens of times more participants than formal Yunarmiya membership. This reflects the functional division of labour: the mass network creates a reserve and regular contact; narrower organisations provide deeper training.

Key trends

The age of entry is falling. Ideological work now begins at preschool age, while organisational involvement starts in primary school.

Part of military preparation has moved into mandatory education through OBZR and training camps.

Functions are distributed across structures: mass coverage through Eaglets of Russia and Movement of the First; applied game-based training through Zarnitsa; permanent organisation through Yunarmiya; advanced training through VOIN.

Modern military specialisations are expanding: UAVs, combat engineering, tactical medicine, communications and reconnaissance.

Permanent infrastructure is growing: Yunarmiya Houses, OBZR classrooms, cadet and agency-profile classes, and VOIN branches.

The occupied territories are embedded in federal programmes rather than treated as a peripheral system. Children participate in the same camps, contests and finals as participants from Russian regions.

Mass structures are growing faster than formal membership in military organisations. This mass layer is what gives the system its scale.

Conclusions

By 2026, the Russian model in the occupied territories has the characteristics of a mature vertical of military-ideological socialisation. It does not require every child to become a Yunarmiya member or VOIN trainee. Schools and mass youth movements provide repeated exposure to Russian military-historical narratives, while specialised programmes select a smaller group for deeper preparation.

The most significant change compared with 2022-2023 is not simply higher participation but a different architecture. Military-patriotic activity has moved from isolated clubs to a system in which mandatory schooling, youth movements, security agencies and specialised centres operate at adjacent stages of one pathway.

For future CSO monitoring, the following categories should be tracked separately: 1) mass school coverage; 2) formal membership; 3) participation in applied military competitions; 4) completion of specialised training; 5) permanent training infrastructure. This avoids double counting while preventing the scale from being understated by focusing only on Yunarmiya or VOIN.
