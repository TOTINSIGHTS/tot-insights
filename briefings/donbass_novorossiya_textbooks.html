History Written Backwards: The "Donbass and Novorossiya" Textbooks for Occupation Schools

TOT Insights and the Centre for the Study of Occupation. October 2026.
Key findings
  • Russia has produced a regional history course, Donbass and Novorossiya: History of Our Region, for children aged about 10 to 13 in the occupied territories: one textbook for Year 5 and one for Years 6 and 7, published in Moscow in 2026 with the exclusive rights held by the Russian Federation. Read together, the two books are a single three-year programme that ends with the region's "return to Russia" in 2022.
  • The books rarely deny facts outright. They remove the links between them. Kyiv stays the capital of Rus, but its inheritance passes to Moscow; the Zaporozhian Sich is called a "Cossack republic", but its Cossacks are "Russian settlers" who "spoke Russian"; and the Sich's destruction by the Russian Empire in 1775 is never described, only its "former" lands.
  • Ukraine enters as something imposed from outside: first as a contemptuous Polish name meaning "borderland", then as a Bolshevik republic into which the region was placed "against the will of its inhabitants", then as a "self-styled" state turned into an "anti-Russia". Ukrainian-language schooling is presented as violence against a Russian region.
  • The end of the story is announced on the first pages and everything before it is selected as proof. End-of-chapter questions build the required conclusion into the question: pupils are asked to "prove" that soldiers of the war against Ukraine are worthy of the heroes of the Second World War.
The textbooks in full Both textbooks are published on TOT Insights as primary sources, in full and unaltered, so that what occupation schools are given to teach can be read and cited directly.

The Russian state has written a local history for the regions it has occupied. Donbass and Novorossiya: History of Our Region, by A. P. Bogdanov and V. N. Ilyin, covers the past of Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson oblasts from the Stone Age to 2022 in two volumes: a 248-page textbook for Year 5 and a 280-page textbook for Years 6 and 7. Both were published in Moscow in 2026 and state that the exclusive rights belong to the Russian Federation. The versions examined here leave blank the date and number of the Ministry of Education order admitting them for use, which suggests they are pre-approval or first-print files from July 2026, ahead of the 2026 to 2027 school year.

This briefing is based on two analytical notes and a joint synthesis by the Centre for the Study of Occupation, and on its matrix of more than sixty quotations. TOT Insights has checked the quotations below against the textbooks; page numbers are the printed page numbers, so each quotation can be found in the full texts. Translations from the Russian are our own.

One course in six stages

The two books do not work as separate texts. Year 5 builds a genealogy that runs Rus, Moscow, Russia, and fits the Cossacks into a "Russian" world. Years 6 and 7 carry the same logic through the imperial, Soviet and present-day periods.

III. The CossacksYear 5, pp. 192–228The Sich is a "Cossack republic", but its Cossacks are Russian-speaking and Orthodox; Pereiaslav is "reunification".Builds Ukrainian Cossackdom into Russian state history.

Rus claimed, not denied

The Year 5 book does not erase Kyiv. It states plainly that Kyiv was one of the two political centres of early Rus and, from 882, its capital. What it does is change the name of the subject. "Kyivan Rus" barely appears; instead the text speaks of "Rus", the "Russian state" and the "Russian lands", so that the Muscovite principality can later join the same line without explanation. The lands of Kyiv and Volhynia under the Grand Duchy of Lithuania become simply "Russian lands", while the Galician-Volhynian and Lithuanian-Ruthenian traditions are given no role as alternative heirs of Rus.

«Столицей Руси стал Киев.»"Kyiv became the capital of Rus."Year 5, p. 100
«Он превратил Москву в центр русской духовности во главе с митрополитом всея Руси...»"He turned Moscow into the centre of Russian spirituality, headed by the Metropolitan of All Rus..."Year 5, p. 155

Together the two statements make a smooth handover: Kyiv is the beginning, Moscow the next centre of the same "Russian" history. A Ukrainian line of succession between them is never needed to explain anything.

"Ukraine" as a foreign insult

The clearest single example is on page 216 of the Year 5 book. The name "Ukraine" is introduced not as the historical name of a territory or a people but as a contemptuous Polish label, and Ukrainians as people the Poles treated as animals. Ukrainian identity is given no origin of its own; it is a name stuck on from outside.

«Те презрительно называли области, населённые русскими православными, Украиной, то есть окраиной.»"They contemptuously called the regions inhabited by Russian Orthodox people 'Ukraine', that is, the borderland."Year 5, p. 216
«Украинцев за людей они не считали, относясь к ним как к рабам, говорящему скоту.»"They did not consider Ukrainians to be people, treating them as slaves, as talking cattle."Year 5, p. 216

The same motif returns in the twentieth century, where Ukraine becomes a Bolshevik administrative construction and then a Western geopolitical project.

The Cossacks without a Ukrainian state

The Year 5 book describes the Zaporozhian Sich in some detail and calls it a "Cossack republic", with its own elected officers and assemblies. But it identifies the Zaporozhians as "Russian settlers" and makes speaking Russian a condition of being a Cossack. The Sich's self-government is acknowledged; it simply leads nowhere Ukrainian.

«Испокон веков в казаки принимали всех, кто был честен и храбр, говорил по-русски...»"From time immemorial anyone who was honest and brave and spoke Russian was accepted into the Cossacks..."Year 5, p. 192
«Она стала центром казачьей республики — Запорожской Сечи.»"It became the centre of a Cossack republic, the Zaporozhian Sich."Year 5, p. 195

The book then sets the Don Cossacks against the Zaporozhians as a lesson in loyalty: the Don Cossacks are disciplined and faithful to the throne, the Zaporozhians unreliable. The desirable historical behaviour is early service to Moscow.

«Донцы, в отличие от запорожцев, сразу избрали служение Русскому государству.»"The Don Cossacks, unlike the Zaporozhians, chose at once to serve the Russian state."Year 5, p. 200

The Khmelnytsky uprising of 1648 is called a war of liberation, but its subject is "the Russian people of Little Russia" (Malorossiya), not a new political community. Pereiaslav in 1654 is the restoration of an earlier unity. The book concedes that the Zaporozhian Host "received broad autonomy", but the autonomy is a detail inside a reunification already complete.

«Так началось воссоединение русских земель.»"Thus began the reunification of the Russian lands."Year 5, p. 222

The missing year: 1775

In the Years 6 and 7 book the destruction of the Sich disappears. Pupils read that the New Sich "existed until 1775" and, three sections later, that in 1775 the lands of the "former" Zaporozhian Sich were added to the Novorossiya province. Between the two sentences the act that made the Sich "former", its liquidation by Russian troops on Catherine II's orders, is never described. The consequence is there; the agent and the act are not.

«Здесь они основали Новую Сечь (существовала до 1775 г.).»"Here they founded the New Sich (it existed until 1775)."Years 6–7, p. 21
«В 1775 г. в Новороссийскую губернию включили владения бывшей Запорожской Сечи.»"In 1775 the possessions of the former Zaporozhian Sich were included in the Novorossiya province."Years 6–7, p. 43

Earlier destruction is handled the same way. Mazepa "planned to betray Peter I"; the Cossacks of the Chortomlyk Sich "gave in to his promises"; and "in response" Russian regiments razed their fortresses and the Sich. Having already called incorporation into Russia "beneficial", the book turns the destruction of Cossack centres into a matter of putting the steppe in order.

Novorossiya and the myth of the empty land

The chapter "The Birth of Novorossiya" opens with a question that contains its answer: how did "deserted steppes" become a flourishing province in a few decades? Yet the same book has just described Cossack winter settlements, trade routes, administrative districts and crafts. The emptiness is not a finding; it is a frame, and it serves to legitimise imperial settlement. A few pages later Russian expansion is placed outside the category of colonisation altogether.

«Как пустынные степи на южных окраинах России за несколько десятилетий превратились в процветающую губернию?»"How did the deserted steppes on Russia's southern borderlands turn into a flourishing province in a few decades?"Years 6–7, p. 40
«Европейские государства приходили на новые земли, чтобы покорять и порабощать, и только Россия — чтобы поддерживать и просвещать.»"European states came to new lands to conquer and enslave, and only Russia came to support and to enlighten."Years 6–7, p. 57

The Ukrainian period as an error to be corrected

The Years 6 and 7 book announces its conclusion on page 5, before any evidence is presented: the region "returned" to Russia two and a half centuries ago, was placed in the Ukrainian Soviet republic "against the will of its inhabitants", and Russia was "forced" to begin the "special military operation" in 2022. The rest of the book serves as retrospective justification.

«...земли Новороссии и большей части Донбасса против воли жителей были включены в состав одной из советских республик — Украинской.»"...the lands of Novorossiya and most of Donbass were, against the will of their inhabitants, included in one of the Soviet republics, the Ukrainian one."Years 6–7, p. 5

The Ukrainian state of 1917 to 1921 is never given a neutral name; it is the "nationalist regime in Kyiv" with its foreign "patrons". The Ukrainisation of the 1920s, in reality part of the Soviet-wide policy of indigenisation, is narrowed to violence by a Ukrainian language against a Russian population, and Ukrainian schooling is said to have held back education.

«После включения в состав УССР Донбасс и Новороссия подверглись украинизации.»"After their inclusion in the Ukrainian SSR, Donbass and Novorossiya were subjected to Ukrainisation."Years 6–7, p. 196
«Насильственное насаждение украинского языка вызвало сопротивление населения нашего края.»"The forcible imposition of the Ukrainian language provoked resistance from the population of our region."Years 6–7, p. 198

Independent Ukraine appears under the scornful heading "In 'self-styled' Ukraine" (samostiinaya). Economic decline, language policy, historical memory and foreign policy are assembled into one causal chain in which Kyiv, under Western and nationalist pressure, tears the region away from Russia. The two books lock together here: Year 5 has already presented Mazepa as a traitor, so Ukraine's different view of him in Year 7 becomes "falsification".

«Власти в Киеве под давлением Запада и националистов делали всё, чтобы их разорвать и превратить Украину в анти-Россию.»"The authorities in Kyiv, under pressure from the West and the nationalists, did everything to break these ties and turn Ukraine into an anti-Russia."Years 6–7, p. 244

The final paragraphs do not describe events so much as prescribe how to judge them: 2014 is a "coup d'état", the protests are the "Russian Spring", the Ukrainian government is the "Kyiv regime", and the last section is titled "Return to Russia".

«Исторические земли России, Донбасс и Новороссия навсегда вернулись в её состав...»"The historical lands of Russia, Donbass and Novorossiya, have returned to its fold for ever..."Years 6–7, p. 270

Why it matters

The most damaging feature of the course is not any single false statement but a repeated structure of causation: Russia is always the actor, and Ukraine is an object, an error or a foreign project. Four techniques carry it. Local history (Mariupol, Zaporizhzhia, Kherson, Donbass, familiar place names, archaeological sites, Cossack stories) is folded into Russian state history, which makes the larger narrative feel closer to home and less obviously ideological. Complex political conflicts become moral oppositions: loyalty and betrayal, liberation and occupation, enlightenment and nationalism, return and separation. The political vocabulary of the present war is projected back onto the seventeenth, eighteenth and twentieth centuries. And the questions and creative tasks make the pupil formulate and "prove" the required interpretation in their own words.

The intended result for a child is simple: the region was never, and could never have been, part of an independent Ukrainian history, and its natural path always led to Russia. That continuity, rather than any one phrase, is the course's central product.

These are not books a parent can opt out of. Occupation schools may teach only from textbooks on the Russian federal list (see the federal textbook and literature lists), and this course is written for them. As an occupying power, Russia is bound by international humanitarian law to keep the existing institutions for children's education working, not to replace them; and under the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child, which Russia has ratified, education must develop respect for a child's own cultural identity, language and values. A state-commissioned course that teaches Ukrainian children that their nation is a foreign insult, their language a form of violence and their country's destruction a homecoming is documentary evidence of the policy of erasing Ukrainian identity in the occupied territories.

Scope of this analysis The analysis concentrates on how the books build their narrative: what they select, leave out and rename, and how their questions work. It is not an external fact-check of every historical claim, and not every quotation is false in itself; several are accurate statements used as anchor points in a larger scheme. The files examined do not carry the number and date of the Ministry of Education's admission order, so it is not yet confirmed which edition reached classrooms in September 2026.
